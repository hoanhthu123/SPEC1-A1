LOOK INSIDE THE WITLING


ABOUT THIS PROJECT

"The way we tend to use resources is we extract them, we turn them into short-life products and then dispose of them. . . In ecosystems, the waste from one organism becomes the nutrient for something else in that system" Pawlyn, M. (2010), ‘Using Nature’s Genius in Architecture’, TEDSalon London.


Michael Pawlyn, a British architect specialising in biomimicry and sustainable design, mentioned in a TEDtalk challenges the linear approach ciricular we design, consume, and manage resources compared to our nature design, consume, and manage resources. His statement shed light on a motto "nature as the model and human development as the part of this model" for interconnection, resource circulation and regeneration. This idea aligns with the Sustainable Development Goal 11 set by the United Nations: Sustainable Cities and Communities, which calls for cities and human settlements to become inclusive, safe, resilient, and sustainable, but not simply about building better cities, but about reflect on how urban systems function and coexist with the natural environment. When these messages are overlooked, sustainability is easily misunderstood as an action for human-consuming less and reducing damage, not a system where people, infrastructure, resources and nature support one another. This project demonstrates the similarity between human system and ecosystem development to vindicate that rather than function as an isolated system, the living city should function as an interconnected living ecosystem, where resources circulate, communities cooperate and systems adapt to environmental changes. Learn from nature, leave no waste behind, repurpose what is already built is the key to design systems that can continuously support life.
